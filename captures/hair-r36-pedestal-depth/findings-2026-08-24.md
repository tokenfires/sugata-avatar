## 2. 🎯 What the failure teaches, and it is worth the round

**Slide 39's fake is not the mud. It is the main thing fighting the mud.** Its isoluminant factor
`(C/luma)^(1−Shadow)` pumps fibre-warm chroma at ZERO energy cost across the groom; strip it (any
honest per-channel form, any input) and the level-matched hair collapses from 0.829 to ~0.43–0.48
warm saturation. The fake is **load-bearing for saturation**: without it the groom would be nearly
grey, and the muddiness — already 38% below reference *with* the fake — is a deficit the fake is
partially masking, not causing.

**And the rim is worth 7% at plate level, not the world.** `rim0` reads +0.058 on a base of 0.829.
R35's "the rim pollutes the pedestal through 26 cards" is true *of the term in its model* and small
*on the plate*.

### Why R35's arithmetic pointed the other way — re-opened, with named suspects

R35 modelled the shipped term's per-light weights as `intensity · colour · wrap · visibility` and
got shipped-term saturation 0.384 against the true-n arm's 0.804. The plate inverts that ordering.
The model omitted, in rising order of suspicion: the panels' real **solid-angle** weighting
(`directRectArea` — the rig's panels are large and close), the key SpotLight's **shadow-map**
attenuation, and any correlation between the **sheet's UV sampling** and the visible strand
population. Which of these carries the inversion is **UNRESOLVED** — R35 §4 warned "term
arithmetic, not a plate," and this is that caveat collecting its debt. The R35 findings stand as
term-model results; their plate-level projection is withdrawn.

## 3. 🔴 Where this leaves "muddy" — the frame shifts

| **chromatic pedestal (R35→R36)** | **refuted at plate level, this round** |

What survives as the mechanism for a 38%-below-reference saturation, given the fake is *pumping*
warmth and the rim costs only 7%: **achromatic dilution**. The R lobe (~35% of the direct groom) is
achromatic by construction and takes the lights' near-white colour; the indirect/GTAO composite
(~10%) is grey; both pour unsaturated light over a warm base. The reference's hair earns its
saturation from real deep scattering; ours earns it from the fake and then dilutes it. **The next
question is a delivery/dilution audit — how much of a hair pixel is achromatic, per term, against
the reference's implied split — not another chroma pump.** That is a measuring round, cheap, and
its operator already exists (`unit-bsdf` and the lobe-share machinery).
