# R34 — a stopwatch that refuses, what refusing established, and the number it will not bless

**Date:** 2026-08-24 · **Tool:** `tools/critic/frame-cost.mjs` (52 selftest gates)
**Registrations:** `2026-08-23-frame-cost-preregistration.md` (**closed VOID**) ·
`2026-08-24-frame-cost-v2-preregistration.md` (**voided on G1**)

Seven calibrated runs across two registrations. **Every one refused to report a cost.** No cost below
is blessed, and none is carried into a decision.

That is not a null result. The refusals established six things that were not known yesterday, killed
two confident hypotheses of mine, and produced the first internally consistent set of groom costs
this project has had.

---

## 1. 🎯 The measurement primitive is licensed on hardware

`visible = false` on a hair page is **measurably indistinguishable** from a page that never had a
groom. Null N1, with census deltas of exactly **0 draws / 0 triangles** on every run:

| run | `cardsA-` vs bald | `cardsB-` vs bald |
|---|---:|---:|
| v1 · 1 | +0.081 | −0.151 |
| v1 · 2 | +0.044 | +0.029 |
| v1 · 3 | +0.083 | +0.021 |

The groom's meshes carry `castShadow = true` (`alive.js:2560-2566`) and three culls an invisible mesh
from every pass, so the toggle drops the G-buffer draw and the shadow draw together. That was an
argument. It is now a measurement.

## 2R. 🔴 Why §2 is retracted — three refutations, all from data that was already on disk

### (a) The decisive control existed, unrun, in the next file

`frame-cost-cards.json` holds **two pages opened from the identical URL** — `cardsA` and `cardsB`,
same `alive(&hair=1)`, **no ribbon geometry on either**, only **three** resident contexts:

| page | hidden p50 |
|---|---:|
| `bald` | 8.866 |
| `cardsB-` | 9.145 |
| `cardsA-` | **11.208** |

**Two identical pages differ by 22.56%**; the three-page spread is **26.41%** against §2's headline
29.60%. **So 26 of the 29.6 points reproduce with both of §2's named causes absent.** And paired
within tick those same two pages are indistinguishable — `cardsB- − cardsA-` reads **+0.148**, sign
53.3%, z 1.03, CI **[−0.159, +0.378]** — passing *both* halves of the registered null gate.

### (b) 🔴 §2 is an unpaired subtraction of one page from another, in the same breath as condemning one

Measured with **this document's own registered statistic** — median of per-tick paired differences —
the page effect is:

| | paired p50 | sign | CI |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `bob11408-` vs bald | **−0.030** | 47.0% | [−0.130, +0.058] |
| `bob4960-` vs bald | **−0.148** | 44.5% | [−0.489, +0.044] |
| `cards-` vs bald | +0.757 | 76.5% | [+0.540, +1.202] |
| `crop8832-` vs bald | +1.039 | 62.5% | [+0.236, +1.895] |

**Two of four are negative and span zero.** The largest paired effect is **15.7%, not 29.6%.**

And the 29.6% is largely a **clock-mixture artefact**: in the slow state all five conditions agree to
**0.56%** (12.976–13.049), and the pooled ordering is exactly the fast-share ordering
(88.5 / 85.5 / 82.5 / 80.5 / 74.0%). That is the identical defect §4(d) indicts — *"a fast-state-only
artefact, invisible to a pooled figure"* — committed on the very next page.

### (c) The stated mechanism is contradicted, and the design could not have tested it

*"A page holding ribbon geometry contends differently even with that geometry hidden."* The two pages
with the **most** geometry, `bob11408` (771,231 visible triangles) and `bob4960` (384,351), have
paired effects of **−0.030 and −0.148 ms** — nothing. Correlation of hidden p50 with resident
triangles is **r = +0.175**. In the ribbons run the page holding **no geometry at all is the most
expensive one**. Resident geometry is the variable that does *not* predict the effect.

🔴 **And page identity was perfectly confounded with PREDECESSOR identity, by construction.**
`tickSchedule` rotated arm order, and **a rotation preserves adjacency exactly** — every arm had
exactly two possible predecessors, fixed by its index, in an 80/20 split, in every tick of every run.
The natural experiment is in the record: between two runs `crop8832`'s predecessor changed from a
one-condition arm to a three-condition arm, and it is **the one page whose cost moved 30 points**
while every other page moved 4–8.

Fixed at `frame-cost.mjs` — a seeded per-tick shuffle gives every arm every predecessor, and the new
gate checks **adjacency** rather than position. `bald` also gains a second condition, because a
one-condition arm's read *always* follows a page switch while a three-condition arm's middle read
never does, and `bald` is the denominator of every cross-page comparison.

### What survives

**Cross-page pooled comparison is unreliable — and that is now stronger, not weaker.** Two pages at
the *identical URL* differ 22.56% on a pooled median while being indistinguishable when paired. The
self-difference primitive and §1's licence are untouched: they never used that channel. What is gone
is the number **30%**, the noun **page identity**, and the mechanism **resident geometry**.

## 3. 🔴 G1 is anti-correlated with the effect it exists to protect

v2's pair-integrity gate drops pairs whose two members fall in different clock-state bins. Measured
across the ribbon run, sorted by effect size:

| pair | workload | agreement | effect |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `bob4960` − vs + | different | 62.5% | +3.275 |
| `crop8832` − vs + | different | 57.5% | +5.182 |
| `bob11408` − vs + | different | **45.5%** | **+7.513** |
| `bald` vs `bob4960-` | **identical** | 88.0% | (null) |
| `bald` vs `crop8832-` | **identical** | 87.0% | (null) |
| `crop8832-` vs `bob11408-` | **identical** | 81.0% | (null) |

Identical-workload pairs sit at **81.0–89.0%** whatever the arm; different-workload pairs degrade in
lockstep with how large the cost is. A groom that costs enough pushes its frame across the bin
boundary, and G1 reads that as a broken pair.

⚠️ **"Perfectly monotone" over three points is worth p = 0.167 by exact permutation — on its own, no
evidence at all.** The claim is nevertheless right, and the data supports it far better than this
table did: **pooling all 81 pairs across the three runs gives ρ = −0.835, p < 5×10⁻⁶.** The original
table also listed three of six available identical-workload pairs; the omitted three read 89.0%,
81.0% and 86.0%, so the stated ceiling was a point low. (The omission flattered the claim.)

**So the bigger the real effect, the more confidently G1 declares the measurement invalid.** That is
a fatal design defect, and it is why every v2 run voided.

⚠️ **And I got this wrong first, on an underpowered test.** Seeing G1 fail on the cards arm I proposed
exactly this contamination — then tested it on the cards arm alone and measured 88.3% workload-free
against 88.8% contaminated, a −0.4 point gap, and **recorded the hypothesis as refuted**. A 1.4 ms
effect against a 9 ms state gap almost never crosses the boundary, so the cards case had no leverage.
The ribbon arms, at +3 to +7.5 ms, have plenty. **The same lesson as Amendment 1: a test without
power does not return "no", it returns nothing.**

## 4. Four instrument defects, each caught by a gate, each previously invisible

**(a) A drift gate firing on its own sampling noise.** Shuffling tick labels destroys every time
relationship while preserving the distribution, so a drift detector must then fire at ~0%. It fired
at **9–15%** in a region whose reference varied 0.9% end to end, and up to **38%** elsewhere. A p50 of
thirteen samples from a bimodal distribution is not a stable location estimate.

**(b) …and simultaneously blind.** On a range containing a documented regime change — minimum
collapsing 12.73 → 4.04 ms — the two medians read **13.04 → 12.99**, 0.4% apart, well inside the 2% it
allowed. A median cannot see a fast mode that never reaches half the mass. **Wrong in both directions
at once.** Replaced by two-sample KS with a permutation null, validated on a pre-stated three-way
test: 4–7% on shuffled data, p = 0.0005 on the real event, quiet on the stable region.

**(c) A separation floor cannot tell a clock state from a k-means artefact.** A 2-means split of a
*unimodal* sample scores **2.62** (gaussian), **3.39** (uniform), **3.42** (skewed) — and the real
captured run scores only **4.40**, because real modes carry wide within-mode spread. The populations
overlap on that axis, so no separation floor works. The **ratio** separates them: artefacts ≤ 1.08,
real states 1.33–2.24. Gated at 1.25.

**(d) 🎯 G0 caught a bias the pooled null concealed.** N1 for `cardsA` read **+0.316 pooled**, which
splits into **+0.607 fast** and **+0.082 slow** — a fast-state-only artefact, invisible to a pooled
figure, found by the gate written to check the state machinery itself.

### 🎯 The one comparison the registered rule blesses

`bob11408` and `bob4960` are the same groom at two densities, and their references are **0.44%
apart** — inside `REFERENCE_TOLERANCE`, so they may be differenced:

> **2.30× the strands, 2.23× the cost.** 11,408 against 4,960 strands; +6.923 against +3.099 ms.

⚠️ **THE RATIO IS NOT RESOLVED TO THE PRECISION IT IS QUOTED AT.** Bootstrapping the ratio itself
gives a 95% CI of **[1.93, 2.52]** — ±13%, a width 26% of the point estimate — and the CI on the
per-1k-strand *difference* **spans zero** at [−0.11, +0.06] ms/1k. So **proportionality is not
established; it is merely not refuted.** The ratio is also not robust to the location statistic:
mean gives 1.84, 20% trimmed gives 2.07, a spread as wide as the CI. And the numerator's pairs
straddle the clock boundary at 58% against the denominator's 30.5%, so the two halves of the ratio
are contaminated at very different rates by exactly the artefact §3 describes.

It remains the best-controlled comparison here — one clock, references 0.438% apart — but "the one
number not weakened" was too strong.

### Every other pair is REFUSED, by the rule, and correctly

References 13.4–29.2% apart. **No parity call is made.** The decision rule is "parity with today's
cards" and it cannot be evaluated across pages whose identical pictures differ by 30%.

## 7. Why nothing here is blessed

**Every run voided.** A number computed after a gate refused is not a number the registration
blessed. v2's own change clause predicted the consequence:

> *"If v2 also fails, the answer is not a v3 — it is that this machine cannot resolve a 2 ms effect,
> which would itself be the finding."*

**That prediction is refuted by its own data.** Sign tests of 98–100%, intervals excluding zero, two
independent pages 3.0% apart, and a 2.23×/2.30× scaling result. The machine resolves it easily. What
failed is §3's gate, for the reason in §2 above — and that is a better finding than the one predicted.

⚠️ **The fix is not a third amendment at two in the morning.** The pattern this project exists to
prevent is loosening a gate after each failure until a number gets through.

| | under rotation | under a decorrelated schedule |

Same tool, same machine, same registered thresholds, one line of scheduling changed.

### 🔴 And then the same defect surfaces a third time, in a third place

Every paired null now passes — including `cards-` against `bald` at **−0.086 ms**. Yet their *pooled*
references read **8.806 against 7.090**, 24% apart, and `REFERENCE_TOLERANCE` refuses to let them be
compared. The two statistics disagree about the same pair of pages, and only one of them is paired.

Measured across the five identical pictures:

| | spread |
|---|---:|
| pooled p50 | **29.7%** |
| within the fast state | 8.5% |
| **within the slow state** | **0.8%** (13.023–13.129) |

The pooled ordering tracks fast share exactly — `cards-` lowest at 65.5% fast and dearest pooled;
`bob11408-` highest at 82.0% and cheapest.

**So all three of this round's failures are one defect wearing three hats:**

| where | the pooled statistic | the paired one that existed |
|---|---|---|
| §2's retracted headline | pooled p50 across pages | the registered per-tick paired median |
| §3's gate **G1** | bins a pooled quantity, so effect size crosses the bin | within-pair state agreement at fixed workload |
| §6's **`REFERENCE_TOLERANCE`** | pooled median of a mixture | **N2 — which is already computed, and passed 6/6** |

⚠️ **A v3 therefore has both replacements already built and validated in this round's own data.** It
would be a *narrowing* — deleting two uses of a statistic proven unfit — rather than a loosening.
**That is still the owner's call and is deliberately not taken here.**

## 8. What a v3 needs

1. **Delete G1 as specified.** §3 shows it measures effect size. Replace it with the workload-free
   probe already in the data: a second read of the *hidden* condition in the same batch, which
   measures clock stability with the workload held constant.
2. **Take the cross-page nulls off the blessing path.** N1 licensed the primitive and has done its
   job; N2 measures page-identity artefact, which §2 shows is a real 30% effect and not something a
   0.25 ms ceiling can gate. The cost channel does not use that channel, so its blessing must not
   depend on it. **N3 is the cost channel's null, and it has never failed.**
3. **One page per run, or accept the reference spread as a stated limit.** Six resident contexts cost
   30% on an identical picture. Parity across grooms needs either serial single-page runs tied
   together by a shared clock probe, or an explicit statement that only same-reference pairs compare.
4. **Keep G0.** It caught something nothing else did.
