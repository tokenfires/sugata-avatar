## 2. What the numbers say, in one paragraph

R36 guessed dilution; R37 measures it. **The primary highlight lobe — achromatic by physics, own
chroma 0.058 — carries ~45% of the hair's light and removing it raises warm saturation by 60%.**
TRT, the lobe that was supposed to be the coloured counterweight, contributes **zero mass** on this
rig (REQ-064 already measured why: nothing lights it). The fake is not just load-bearing, it is the
*only* warm source, fighting an R-wash twice its chroma-weighted size. The indirect bundle is a
small second diluter.

## 3. ⚠️ Interpretation — beyond the registered round, labelled as such

An achromatic R is correct physics; the reference's hair has one too and stays saturated. The
difference cannot be R's *existence* — it must be R's *distribution*. Ours delivers ~45% of the
pixel's light as an achromatic wash **across the entire groom mask**; a reference-grade primary
band is *narrow* — high where the band is, near-zero elsewhere. That is the same defect the
declared-red gates have been failing for weeks from the other side: **"the hair's OWN dynamic range
reaches 4.0:1" is red because R has no peak.** One mechanism now explains both symptoms:

> **R's energy is spread flat instead of peaked. Where the band should be there is not enough
> highlight; everywhere else its achromatic floor washes out the fibre's warmth.**

The muddiness and the missing band would then be one problem, and the lever is R's *shape* —
its longitudinal width/shift and the tangent field that spreads it — not its weight (turning R down
uniformly dims the band that is already too weak). That is the next round's hypothesis to register,
and it is falsifiable on the same operators: narrow the band, and BOTH the dilution marginal and
the dynamic-range gate must move together.
