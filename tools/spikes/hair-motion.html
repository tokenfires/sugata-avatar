Spike 6.6/9.14 — can the groom move, at 60 Hz, inside the frame budget?

The shipped groom is 294 cards of 17 rings (measured off assets/hair/bob01/g050.glb: 10,648 vertices, 296 connected components — 294 of exactly 34 and 2 scalp shells of 326). Every card is already a ribbon around a guide curve, so the guide layer this simulation needs is 4,998 particles, not the 776k TressFX reports for a strand groom. This page runs a real Dynamic Follow-The-Leader solver in a TSL compute pass and asks three separate questions with three separate numbers:

  1. Where does the cost live? Sweep the chain count from 1 to 65,536 at a fixed substep count. A chain count of 1 is not a groom — it is the price of the dispatch itself, and the slope above it is the price of the hair.
  2. What do substeps cost? Sweep 1–8 substeps at the shipped chain count. Each substep is another dispatch, so this is the fixed cost measured a second way.
  3. Does it matter how the dispatches are submitted? Every variant runs twice: once with renderer.compute() called per kernel, which opens a WebGPU compute pass each time, and once with the whole frame handed over as one array, which three.js runs inside a single pass. WebGPU tracks the read-after-write hazards between dispatches in a pass itself, so the ordering DFTL needs survives either way.
  4. Does the GPU earn its place? The same solver, and a VRM-faithful spring chain, run on the CPU at the shipped size. If the CPU number fits, the compute path is a complication rather than a capability.

Correctness, read back off the solver's own buffer

A cost is only worth reading if the thing being costed is doing its job. After 600 simulated frames of the head shake, the positions are read back from the GPU and checked against what DFTL claims: every segment still its rest length (that is the whole point of Follow-The-Leader — one pass, exact, no iteration count to tune), the tips actually moved, and nothing is inside the skull. Append ?breakFtl=1 to remove the projection line and nothing else; the length row goes red and the other two do not, which is what makes this a check rather than a decoration.

3 — CPU baselines at the shipped groom size

Wall-clock over the solve only, performance.now(), no rendering. The spring-bone row is the three-vrm update transcribed to flat arrays — inertia (1 − dragForce), stiffness along the bone axis, gravity, then the bone-length normalisation that is its distance constraint — with the fixed 60 Hz timestep punch-list 6.6 asks for and three-vrm does not have.
