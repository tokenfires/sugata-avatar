Save and copy serialize supported `Avatar.create()` options from the attached runtime report, including the currently worn outfit, foundation, hair, framing, light and seed. They do not serialize an unfulfilled clothing request. Export is disabled while dressing and refuses partial/retired states. A rejected clothing transaction retains the current look; a rendering failure after successful dressing enters the unavailable state and does not claim the old clothes remain. Clipboard refusal offers selectable JSON and a download fallback. Camera orbit and motion time are viewing state, not exported identity properties. A consumer can pass the saved object alongside its own canvas:

```js

```

It exposes no unimplemented skin/hair colour, sculpt, age/build or independently mixable top/bottom controls. ## Visual and scope limits

These are integration studies using existing stand-in clothes, not the final authored wardrobe capsule. The page says so. Visible patches remain at the elegant neckline/chest and skirt and at the casual trouser/sock boundary; the shape of a visible patch alone does not prove its exact geometric cause. The sock geometry is part of the shoe fragment. Other body bakes, garment colour changes, hats and wardrobe-agent negotiation are outside this slice. Existing hair opacity/shape and facial-material quality remain visible. This page is not a claim of final visual quality or whole-garment collision clearance.

## Resize and temporal warm-up

Fresh desktop and fresh mobile renders are usable. Desktop-to-mobile resizing restarts temporal sampling and the 16-frame plate showed visible streaks around the head; unchanged beauty converges to a clean image by 80 frames. Rapid full-body/portrait camera changes likewise require temporal warm-up. These observations do not prove a permanent graph/cache defect. Clearing history and recreating the resolve were rejected; the latter also stranded compiled scene uniforms on the original projection matrix. Both experimental page patches were reverted.

A separate first-frame ordering issue is repaired in `Stage.updateViewport()`: the existing scene pass receives the renderer's full drawing-buffer dimensions before TAAU reads them. `PassNode` applies the scene resolution scale once. The temporal resolve and its projection-matrix object stay the same. Four CPU groups exercise actual PassNode/TAAU methods; six actual GPU groups reject the predecessor's old 594×572 jitter input after a 343×625 canvas resize and require the corrected 226×412 input/aspect. They also retain target/temporal identities and texture count, and require identical physical arrays/head/camera/time across old/current/fresh arms. Equal 80-frame resized/fresh views are visually clean (head/upper-torso mean absolute RGB difference 0.004589 in 0–1 units). This change is not claimed to accelerate stochastic-AA warm-up.

## Save the current image

**Save image (PNG)** downloads only the rendered avatar canvas, at its current pixel dimensions, camera view, framing, lighting and attached outfit. **Save settings (JSON)** and **Copy settings** retain the supported configuration workflow.

PNG export briefly holds the page's animation loop and orbit controls. It calls the existing `stage.draw()` and immediately calls `canvas.toBlob()` in the same task; it does not call `Avatar.update()`, `Avatar.step()` or `OrbitControls.update()`. Motion resumes in its previous running/paused mode without catching up the time spent encoding. The image records the current temporal sample, including any existing AA warm-up; no extra settling or resolution increase is implied.

Null/empty/non-PNG encoding, a thrown render/encoder error or a rejected download dispatch reports an image failure. Pending clothing or another image export prevents an overlapping export. The page removes temporary download links, revokes their object URLs, and suppresses an encoding result if its Avatar retires before the callback. Both PNG and JSON URLs are also revoked on page exit.

The focused actual-WebGPU export gate is `node packages/testbed/src/showcase-image.gpu.selftest.mjs --out=/tmp/showcase-image`. Its scope is current-frame image/ownership correctness, not garment fit or a full motion suite.
