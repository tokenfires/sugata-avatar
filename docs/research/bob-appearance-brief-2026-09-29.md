Robert and Cora developed this brief on September 29, 2026 by discussing six supplied reference images. It records Robert's appearance goals and translates them into guidance for a future implementation agent and critic. The primary target is a composed medium bob with continuous curved flow, rounded volume, and subtly asymmetric face framing. Short, wavy, tousled, and curly bobs are additional variations with their own shape requirements.

This is a working design brief for Robert to review. It records desired behavior; it does not establish a new asset, a renderer diagnosis, or a completed implementation. ## Medium bob appearance

### Continuous flow around the whole head

Visible strands and small groups should trace long, gentle paths from the crown toward the ends. They arc outward around the head, fall downward, and turn inward near the jaw or neck. The shared outward curve supplies body; the downward fall gives the impression of weight. Root attachment should feel plausible without requiring visible scalp.

Here, vertical flow means continuous crown-to-tip direction, including sweeping curves. It does not require straight vertical stripes. Preserve several scales: the overall shape, readable groups of strands, and finer striations within those groups. Sparse flyaways and modest differences between neighboring paths provide natural variation.

Layers and overlap can hide individual strands. Judge directional continuity across visible sections rather than demanding that every individual strand be visible all the way from scalp to tip. Within R6, the upper-right and lower-left panels are especially useful smooth-rear examples; its looser middle-center and lower-right panels carry different styling choices.

The rear must participate in the same styling pattern as the sides and front. Robert reports an uneven or messy patch at the back of Sugata's current avatar. Reproduce that appearance before diagnosing it. A smooth-medium candidate should replace the visual discontinuity with coherent flow while retaining strand structure and rounded volume. A featureless panel would not satisfy this goal. References: R1, R3, and the smooth examples in R6.

### Open and slightly asymmetric face framing

Give the front sections complementary placement. One follows the cheek more closely with a gentle curve; the other sweeps back enough to reveal more of the face. The result should feel intentionally styled and inviting. Robert finds the current avatar's approximately equal forward placement too enclosing.

Use the relationship in R2, accounting for its oblique camera angle. Do not infer exact geometry or a mandatory side from screen-space widths. No preferred left or right side has been selected. Shorter face-framing layers may connect the fringe to the longer side sections, as in R3. A full blunt fringe is an optional feature, not a requirement for every bob.

### Smooth volume through the ear region

Build gentle lift near the roots and a rounded outer shape that turns inward toward the tips. In the covered-ear medium examples, the ear sits within this volume. Its location should not be legible as an isolated bump in the outer hair surface.

Robert's fluid analogy describes how fine strands can redistribute around an obstruction while the overall hairstyle retains its shape. It is an appearance and motion intention, not an instruction to model hair as a gas. The future implementation must preserve contact clearance while achieving that outer shape. An intentionally tucked variation may expose an ear, as in R4.

### A tidy cut with small natural differences

Maintain a readable lower contour. Layer endings and individual strand groups can introduce modest unevenness without breaking the overall cut line. Tips should generally turn inward for this medium target. Avoid regularly spaced teeth, abrupt panel ends, conspicuous slots, and a uniformly rigid edge. R1 through R3 show different degrees of layering; none establishes one universal hem height.

### Appearance during movement

Robert wants hair that can look carefully placed at rest, then move slightly out of that arrangement during normal head and body movement. Wind can cause a larger departure. These are distinct viewing conditions. Later moving evidence should show small strand and lock differences within a recognizable hairstyle, with settling that retains its intended character. A still reference cannot qualify those behaviors.

## Optional bob variations

Companions and their users should eventually be able to choose a style family and a few authored variations with recognizable character. The following are design directions, not a claim that these variants are available now.

Cut, placement, and texture are distinct dimensions that can coexist: an angled cut may also be wavy, and a tuck is a placement choice. The examples below describe recognizable combinations rather than mutually exclusive categories.

| Variation | Distinguishing requirements | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smooth medium | Coherent crown-to-tip flow, rounded body, inward tips, subtly offset face framing. | R1 through R3 |
| Short formal | Compact rounded volume, exceptionally tidy nape, intentional longer and shorter front sides, and one side swept or tucked back. Controlled sheen and precision give the formal, expensive appearance Robert described. | R4, especially its lower-right rear view |
| Classic with fringe | Smooth rounded shape and a full fringe. | R5 upper left |
| Lifted and loosely waved | Swept volume and broad loose waves producing the blow-dried appearance Robert described. | R5 upper middle |
| Tousled | Related waves, separated locks, and outward wisps distributed around the shape. | R5 upper right |
| Wavy | Continuous S-shaped locks through sides and back. | R5 lower left |
| Strongly angled | A higher, shorter back leading into longer front sections, with smooth flow. | R5 lower middle |
| Curly | Compact curls, looping ends, and a more irregular outline supported by the larger shape. | R5 lower right |

For the short formal proposal, Robert explicitly wants the asymmetry to be part of the cut. R4 shows the desired visible relationship, but its tucked side contributes to apparent length; the image alone does not isolate the actual cut lengths.

For tousled hair, Robert's description of even wisps is interpreted as a balanced distribution and related scale of variation. Literal equal spacing could look mechanical. The deliberately composed disorder should remain legible through continuous locks and a consistent overall pattern. This does not require the smooth medium variation to acquire waves or rough patches.

The lower-left panel of R5 reminds Robert of Sugata's rear patch. That resemblance does not establish that the image or style caused the existing asset's design. Any causal account requires separate evidence.

## Hair colour choices

Robert requests black, brown, auburn, blonde, red, grey, and white as recognizable base choices, plus coloured hues applied to those bases and adjustment from dark to light. The following control structure is a proposal based on that request.

| Control | Purpose and example |
| --- | --- |
| Base colour | Choose a familiar family such as brown, auburn, blonde, grey, or white. Keep auburn and red separately recognizable because Robert requested both. |
| Lightness | Move within an appropriate range, such as dark brown through medium brown to light brown. The visible control changes hair colour; scene exposure is a separate setting. |
| Tint and amount | Add a warm, cool, or coloured cast with adjustable strength. The exact hue set remains open. |
| Strand colour variation | Optional future control for subtle variation or more visible highlights and lowlights along the strand flow. This is suggested by R6 and awaits Robert's confirmation as a control. |

Lightness is the proposed user-facing name for dark versus light. Luminance is a technical measurement term and is unnecessary in this interaction. Sheen is also a separate appearance property: brighter highlights should not be mistaken for a lighter base colour.

Black and white can serve as useful named endpoints. Avoid confusing labels such as dark white. Their rendered appearance must still include highlights, shading, and possible warm or cool casts. Grey may eventually include a mix of differently pigmented strands; that is an additional design possibility, not an established implementation requirement.

R6 shows why colour cannot be judged as a flat swatch alone. Dark and light paths follow the hairstyle's curves, with differing amounts of colour variation. Require the shape and strand direction to remain readable in dark, intermediate, and pale choices under the same viewing conditions. The photograph's exact contribution from pigment, illumination, and image processing cannot be separated by inspection alone.

Robert also proposed colour as an inspection aid. The [first controlled diagnostic](../evidence/hair-2026-09-29/colour-diagnostic/README.md) supports comparing exact dark, medium brown, and pale material settings on held geometry. Medium brown preserves shadow separation while pale makes some facets easier to see and loses finer contrast. Neither recolouring nor diagnostic lighting is an appearance fix. Require a concrete defect to remain recognizable across relevant views and return to intended colours for candidate assessment.

The Portuguese labels are ordinary words: alegria means joy, paz peace, felicidade happiness, amor love, motivação motivation, carinho affection, criatividade creativity, superação overcoming adversity, and euforia euphoria. The image does not establish whether a salon uses them as product names. Treat them as image labels, not verified dye specifications.

## Instructions for future implementation

1. Start with this medium appearance target and reproduce Robert's three reported issues in current front, side, rear, and oblique captures: the rear patch, ear-region bulges, and enclosing front symmetry. Establish which existing asset will serve as the first candidate. The descriptive word medium does not automatically rename or replace either existing bob ID.
2. Preserve the accepted assets and a reproducible baseline. Work on an isolated candidate. Read the current hair checkpoint before selecting a mechanism; older research documents describe different source states and are not current proof of a cause.
3. Follow visible evidence to the relevant mechanism. Discontinuity could involve geometry, strand direction, shading, transparency, contact, or a combination. A list of possibilities is not a diagnosis. State what each proposed change predicts will improve and which views will reveal a regression.
4. Keep camera, lighting, colour, pose, and motion time matched when comparing a causal change. Evaluate several colour choices in a separate controlled comparison. Preserve reference proportions and style boundaries instead of blending all panels into a single target.
5. Earn progression with visible improvements at ordinary viewing size and at close range. Numerical and contact checks support the comparison; passing them does not establish that the hairstyle looks better. Later assess ordinary movement, settling, and relevant contact conditions on the exact candidate being considered.

## Instructions for the critic

Read the supplied images directly before reviewing a candidate. Use R1 through R3 as the medium target and use the other composites only for the selected variation or colour question. Evaluate the avatar's hair; differences in a reference person's face, makeup, clothing, background, or photographic lighting do not independently establish a hair improvement.

For each finding, identify the reference and panel, the candidate view and region, what is visibly different, and why that difference matters to Robert's stated target. Distinguish observed appearance from a proposed cause. Report improvement, remaining problems, regressions, and anything unassessable from the supplied views.

Robert's overnight steering requires a valid example from actual evidence and two concrete alternatives, with tradeoffs, for every actionable criticism. Retain a decision log. If small changes begin to be undone or redone without new evidence, pause that tuning loop and reconsider the diagnosis, comparison method, or representation.

| Criterion | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Rear continuity | Long, coherent paths through the back with no unexplained change into a rough patch or featureless panel. |
| Rounded body | A smooth outward curve and downward fall, with plausible root attachment and an inward turn near the ends. |
| Face openness | A deliberate difference between front sections that opens the face while preserving framing. |
| Ear region | A continuous outer shape for the covered-ear target, or a coherent tuck for a variation that exposes the ear. |
| Strand grouping | Readable groups and finer striations that cooperate with the overall flow across dark and light areas. |
| Cut contour | A tidy overall boundary with modest natural differences, free of distracting slots or regular teeth. |
| Variation consistency | The selected smooth, wavy, tousled, or curly pattern remains coherent across the whole head. |
| Colour readability | Dark, intermediate, and pale choices retain useful shape and strand information without relying on a single dramatic lighting setup. |
| Moving appearance | Where moving evidence is supplied, the style remains recognizable during small motions and settling; contact and temporal artifacts are reported separately. |

Explain the verdict in ordinary visual language before discussing implementation. Do not call increased smoothness alone a success if it removes the fine structure Robert values. Do not excuse an unintended rear patch by citing a different bob variation. Mark missing views or moving evidence as unassessed, and retain the distinction between a promising appearance candidate and a fully qualified replacement.
