# REQ-063 — a shadowed rim buys exactly what deleting the rim buys, and deleting it is free

**Date:** 2026-08-23 · **Registration:** `55f1711`, committed before any arm was rendered ·
**Tool:** `tools/critic/hair-rimshadow.mjs` · **Plates:** `captures/hair-r33-rimshadow`

REQ-063 asked for a shadow caster on the portrait rim. `captures/hair-r32-glint/rim-shadow.md`
found a lead worth +2.492 codes of hair chroma and this round registered four gates against it
before measuring.

**It fails the gate that decides it, and the reason is sharper than "it does not work".**

---

## 1. The controls, read before the gates

| control | result | what it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| **drift** — one configuration captured twice under two URL spellings | **0.0000 codes** | the run did not drift; a hundredth of a code is signal |
| **null** — `kicker.shadowFraction:1`, the identical code path on a light at irradiance 0.07 | **−0.0030**, ratio **0.001** | the statistic reads the RIM, not "a shadow map was added" |

The null matters more than usual here. Every shadowed arm builds a `SpotLight` with a 4096² map, so
a statistic that responded to *the presence of a caster* rather than to *the rim being occluded*
would have produced exactly the result this round wanted. It does not: a caster on a light at 0.4%
of the rim's irradiance moves the number by three thousandths of a code.

## 2. The sweep, and it is clean

| arm | mass-mean chroma | Δ | top decile | R/B | p50 linear |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped | 33.506 | — | 34.586 | 1.771 | 0.06466831 |
| `shadowFraction 0.25` | 33.950 | +0.443 | 35.200 | 1.825 | 6.445e-2 |
| `shadowFraction 0.50` | 34.507 | +1.000 | 35.845 | 1.883 | 6.421e-2 |
| `shadowFraction 0.75` | 35.188 | +1.682 | 36.522 | 1.949 | 6.401e-2 |
| **`shadowFraction 1.00`** | **35.998** | **+2.492** | 37.245 | **2.022** | **0.06378722** |
| **rim DELETED (ceiling)** | **36.014** | **+2.508** | 37.246 | **2.024** | **0.06378722** |

Monotonic, no threshold, and the lead reproduces exactly: +2.492 codes at `shadowFraction: 1`.

## 3. 🔴 And the last two rows are bit-identical, which is the whole finding

**`shadowFraction: 1` and `rim.irradiance: 0` produce the same p50 luma to eight decimal places —
`0.06378722` both.** Not close: identical.

So the registered bisection for the matched-luma control terminates at **E = 0**, exactly, with the
luma matched to **0.00%** against a registered window of ±0.5%. And then:

> **GATE 1 — beats the free alternative: shadow 2.4917 against matched dim 2.5079 — 0.994×, floor
> 2.0× ❌ FAIL**

**A fully shadowed rim delivers nothing to the hair.** Not "less"; nothing. The groom occludes itself
from a light directly behind it so completely that shadowing that light and deleting it are the same
operation as far as every hair pixel is concerned — and the second one costs nothing while the first
builds a `SpotLight` with a 4096² shadow map that `LightingRig.selftest.mjs` prices at **2.62 ms**,
more than the entire groom's 1.870 ms.

⚠️ **This also converts §8's registered caveat from a risk into a measurement.** The registration
warned that the groom casts the shadow of its **card quads, not its strands**
(`alive.js:2555-2566`), so every shadowed arm would over-occlude. The over-occlusion is not partial.
It is total.

## 4. The other gates, and why gate 4 was not run

- ✅ **GATE 2 — visibility: 2.4917 codes, floor 1.0.** The effect is real and above the 8-bit floor.
- ✅ **GATE 3 — hair-specific: skin moves 0.8369 against hair's 2.4917**, 0.336 of it, ceiling 0.5.
  Not a warming frame.
- ⚪ **GATE 4 — cost: deliberately not measured.** All four had to pass to ship. With gate 1 failed
  the branch is decided, and timing a caster already refused is GPU time on a foregone conclusion —
  the same call REQ-064's gate 3 got, and stated rather than quietly skipped.

## 5. 🎯 REQ-063's "the two changes have to land together" is refuted

The entry's own text says `sideVisibility` goes to 0 with a rim shadow, and `LightingRig.js` adds
*"the two changes have to land together or neither is measurable."* The 2×2 had never been run.

| arm | chroma | Δ | R/B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped | 33.506 | — | 1.771 |
| shadow alone | 35.998 | +2.492 | 2.022 |
| **`sideVisibility` 0 alone** | **41.840** | **+8.334** | **0.932** |
| the pair | 36.057 | +2.551 | 2.015 |

1. **THE PAIR IS THE SHADOW.** +2.551 against +2.492 for the shadow alone — removing
   `sideVisibility` on top of a shadow adds **0.059 codes**, and that is exactly what the physics
   predicts: with the rim occluded there is nothing left for `saturate(wi·wr + 1)` to discard. The
   two changes do **not** have to land together. The shadow does the work and the second half is
   redundant.
2. 🔴 **AND `sideVisibility` 0 WITHOUT A SHADOW IS THE VIOLET, WEARING THE STATISTIC'S CLOTHES.**
   It scores **+8.334 codes — more than three times the winner** — while **R/B collapses from 1.771
   to 0.932**, i.e. the groom goes blue. It is the largest "improvement" in the table and it is the
   defect. See §6.

## 6. 🚩 The blind spot in my own registered statistic, recorded rather than repaired

`chromaInCodes` is the norm of a pixel's departure from its own grey. **It is unsigned.** It cannot
tell warm chroma from cool chroma, so an arm that floods the groom with violet scores as a large
improvement.

That did not change this round's verdict — the winner is drawn from the `shadowFraction` sweep by
construction, and R/B was registered as reported-but-deciding-nothing, so the failure was visible.
**It would have decided a round whose arms included `vis0`.**

⚠️ **Not fixed here, deliberately.** Repairing a statistic after seeing which arm it flatters is the
move `pre-registration-binds-the-registrant` exists to forbid, even when the repair is obviously
right. **The next registration on a colour question should use a signed statistic** — R/B, or chroma
projected onto the fibre's own hue axis (7.1° for `0x1A0E0C`) — and this paragraph is the reason.

## 7. Disposition

**REQ-063 → REJECTED**, on the registered rule as written: *"Gate 1 fails → the rim's wash is real
and the fix is a free constant, not a caster."*

⚠️ **AND THE REGISTERED RULE DOES NOT SEE THE WHOLE DECISION, WHICH IS STATED RATHER THAN USED AS AN
ESCAPE.** Gate 1 compares the two options **on hair only**. Deleting the rim also removes the
figure's separation from the backdrop — R29 confirmed on a render that `rim.irradiance:0` "removes
all separation from the background, so deleting it is not the answer" — while a shadowed rim keeps
it. That difference is real, it is worth something, and **it is not what this round measured**, so
it cannot rescue the verdict. What it does is define the next question:

> The rim's job is backdrop separation. Its cost is a wash over the whole groom. Those are separable
> — and the cheap separable lever is `rim.irradiance` on the **body** preset versus the **portrait**
> preset, not a 2.62 ms caster.

**What this round establishes for whoever takes that on:** the hair's entire exposure to the rim is
worth **2.508 codes** of mass-mean chroma, that figure is the ceiling on every possible rim
intervention, and it is bounded by a plate — `rim0` — rather than argued.

## 8. What is now closed on the rim

Three rounds looked for a rim fix for the hair. All three are answered:

- **Hue** (REQ-078) — rejected 2026-08-23: the outline and the muddiness are different problems, and
  hue is the lever for neither.
- **A shadow caster** (REQ-063) — rejected here: identical to deletion on hair, at 2.62 ms.
- **The pair with `sideVisibility`** — refuted here: the second half adds 0.059 codes.

And the ceiling stands where `hair-r31-norim-ttoff` already put it. **The rim is worth ~2.5 codes to
the hair and the muddiness is 38% of its saturation.** Whatever fixes "muddy" is not the rim, and
after REQ-064 it is not a lobe either. It is the fibre and the per-channel `T_f`.
