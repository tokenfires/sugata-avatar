### Accepted repair: the body and hair now share a capture frame

The motion comparison exposed a real capture defect: the camera, CPU head matrices and GPU hair
centres agreed, while the rendered body did not. In one saved first-frame pair, 9,755 of 9,800
pixels in an unchanged face rectangle differed. Awaiting the initial render alone fixed that
first frame but did not fix later motion frames.

Three's skinning node caches a skeleton upload by renderer frame ID. `hair.html` advanced its
simulation on demand while leaving that renderer ID on the wall-clock rAF loop, so several rapid
steps could reuse the body's old pose. Capture mode now stops that internal loop, advances the
renderer clock for each draw and pins its time to the simulation. Capture controls are also
published after the initial draw finishes. Reset rewinds simulation time while keeping renderer
frame IDs monotonic, so it cannot reuse a stale skeleton upload. Ordinary animation keeps its
normal frame loop.

The corrected comparison has **zero changed face pixels** at all five saved matching states;
comparing different poses changes 9,797 of the same 9,800 pixels. A new actual-WebGPU regression,
`hair-capture-clock.selftest.mjs`, is automatically discovered by the existing runner. It checks
three moving poses across fast and delayed loads/steps, renderer counters and the actual body
bone-upload array. Removing the frame tick leaves stale bone matrices and fails; leaving rAF
running fails the counter oracle. These controls do not rely on the cap candidate.
