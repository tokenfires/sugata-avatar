## Finding 1 — the gain survives restoration, but moving recovery remains unproved

**Actual witnesses:** the neutral right-profile rear corner and both back corners retain the lower-v1 inward turn after the exact 19-card restoration. The fresh dark rear-oblique pair confirms the gain under Studio lighting. However, [failed lower-v1 motion](../lower-motion/CRITIQUE.md) proved 634 head-sequence triangle/body pairs despite positive static/rest evidence and an intact exterior that looked improved. Restoring authoring records does not prove their new live paths equal side v3: the groom-wide median/compliance can change again.

Two concrete alternatives:

1. **Recommended: qualify exact `8c458428…` with the matched head and ordinary protocols, retaining full 338-card/posed-body readbacks, signed-vertex checks and other-card data.** Revisit the original head contact intervals **249–265, 339–349, 386–396**, with intact images near 255/345/390 and returns. Evaluate the remaining whole selected scope, not only restored cards. Tradeoff: this keeps the visible outer gain while testing the actual new combination, but it requires new candidate audits and may expose a different failure. The separate fringe-494/front-373 witness must be interpreted if changed dynamics make it relevant; it is not automatically fixed by this fallback.
2. **Alternative: hold this as a static-positive candidate and continue using side v3 as the moving lead.** Tradeoff: this avoids further qualification work, but the retained inward ends remain unqualified for moving use. It is a valid stop state, not acceptance of the failed lower-v1 motion.

The parent selected the fresh-motion alternative. No moving result is incorporated in this static critique. Neither branch supports another amplitude, width, transition-height or normal pass from these images alone.

## Finding 2 — persistent front/crown and fine hem issues are not evidence of fallback damage

**Actual witnesses:** the neutral front still has short forehead tabs around x295–550/y245–345 and crown breaks near x305–520/y60–150 in all three versions. The rear-oblique lower edge retains small pointed separations; the neutral back lower sweep stays soft rather than forming a newly demonstrated hard crease. These remain short of the reference brief's clean, connected flow, but this comparison does not attribute them to the 19 restored cards.

Two concrete alternatives:

1. **Recommended: retain these witnesses and defer their separate attribution until the moving-contact decision.** The already recorded rendering-only fringe ablation can distinguish the 34-card fringe's contribution later while preserving the complete attached groom. Tradeoff: known appearance imperfections remain, but this avoids mixing a new styling change into the recovery qualification.
2. **Alternative: first project original source/card identities into the visible front/crown regions, then select a bounded rendering ablation.** Tradeoff: it can separate fringe, cap and nonfringe mass/root contributions, but projection alone cannot prove visible ownership through alpha/depth and adds analysis before a causal image. It provides no reason to alter the current fallback now.

## Limits

- Rest checks address the selected hair/body geometry. Garments, hair-to-hair contact, nonselected-card moving contact, alpha visibility and times between samples remain separate questions.

- This review preserves the exact asset and source evidence. Any later candidate or mechanism change needs separate evidence rather than inheriting these conclusions.
