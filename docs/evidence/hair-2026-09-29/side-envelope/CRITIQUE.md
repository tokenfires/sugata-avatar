Original root positions, width magnitudes, topology, UVs, weights and material should be retained as declared controls; root centres remaining fixed must not be mislabeled as root edges remaining fixed.

Compare intact profile, front and opposite-side views against v3 before interpreting a new shape as improvement. Audit every newly selected rest triangle and the preserved-byte boundary. Any eventual motion qualification must measure the changed shared collider and the newly affected front/side behavior rather than borrowing v3's completed motion results.

### B — one supported replacement path for card 382

Replace only the proven offending card's path with a smooth, body-supported guide, preserving its root, width magnitudes and declared endpoint relationship to the face. Compare its intact profile/front result and exact rest clearance. This would be a new path intervention, not the low-value guide-clearance bypass described above.

**Tradeoff:** the attribution is sharp and the scope is much smaller. The removal pair predicts an immediate local benefit if the supported replacement avoids the slash while retaining coverage. However, the already visible upper-ear crossed patch remains outside this intervention, and repeated one-card repairs could become another sequence of marginal local changes without a coherent side-field design.

**Decision:** A is the better next isolated probe. The evidence now includes a proven long-path owner, an adjacent residual visible field, a source reason that the old local stage bypass does not address most of that owner's path, and a successful frozen posterior representation. Stop after one inspectable prototype and its grounded clearance correction, if needed; choose the next action from its actual images and failures.
