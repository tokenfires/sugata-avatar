## Two bounded correction alternatives

1. **Establish a valid resting hand envelope, then derive the native inward allowance from geometry.** Preserve the accepted body and clothing, and use the complete hand/finger shape plus the adjacent forearm, actual retained body surface and garment surface to determine a feasible neutral placement and inward bound. Check crossing and containment rather than optimizing the existing pair count. Any later trial must use normal initialization and real shared posture/yield updates, with the current A prefix retained as its control. This directly addresses the common initial contact and the later inward collapse with a relatively small ownership change. Its tradeoff is reduced inward expressive range and possible dependence on body shape, outfit and finger pose; the current selected faces cannot prove a general bound. No angle should be chosen from these pictures.

2. **Apply a constrained correction to the composed arm chain.** Preserve the owners' expressive intent, then resolve the resulting hand/forearm envelope against the body and clothing at one explicit point after native composition. This can cover interacting posture, gesture and idle contributions more broadly. It requires a defined shoulder/elbow/wrist objective, limits on correction and continuity, and one clear writer; an extra competing bone writer or late inverse of one quaternion term would not be equivalent. The tradeoff is greater solver, temporal and lifecycle complexity, including the risk of sliding, popping or distorted elbows. A future bounded probe should retain contact and no-contact controls and examine continuous replay before broader qualification.

The first route is the more direct next feasibility study for the proved initial-plus-inward failure. The second remains useful if a valid authored envelope cannot preserve the needed expressive range. This is a recommendation for measured design work, not approval of a candidate, numerical angle, cap, asset export or production filter.

## Limits

This independent visual judgment uses still images, not continuous playback. I did not assess transition smoothness, flicker, speed, gesture timing, all 750 images as a sequence, other identities/outfits/seeds, or full-motion safety. Geometry covers discrete selected families and cannot alone detect every enclosed or unselected surface. Existing hair appearance is outside this review and remains deferred. The review qualifies a diagnosis and the next design question, not a repaired avatar.
