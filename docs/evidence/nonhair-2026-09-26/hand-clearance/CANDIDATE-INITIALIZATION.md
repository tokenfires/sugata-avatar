## Sway: a necessary correction to the apparent pose-family dependency

Current `Sway` loads its **own** `relaxed-standing`, `weight-left` and `weight-right` objects in its constructor and caches the relaxed chain's accumulated inverse there (`motion/Sway.js:1542–1560,2967–2994`). Its runtime computes stance deltas from the relaxed anchor and the chosen side via quaternion interpolation and cumulative frames (`:2909–2964`); it does not call `RestPose.blendTo()` on the whole skeleton each frame.

Crucially, `SWAY_CHAIN` explicitly **excludes arms, hands and the head** (`:1115–1156`). The weight-pose JSONs contain upper-arm adjustments, but this layer does not output them. Therefore an arms-only standing change does not create a new Sway shoulder delta, and editing the arm entries in weight-left/right cannot be claimed to improve current Sway hand clearance. The shoulders/hands still move as descendants of the trunk and hips.

There are two real dependencies to preserve:

- If a candidate changes an actual Sway-chain ancestor, changing only the relaxed anchor changes the subtraction against both weight poses. Bind-time pose fields alone are insufficient: `accumulateRelaxedPose()` ran in the constructor. Treat all three exact pose files as one manifest-bound family, explicitly declaring which bytes are unchanged. If the family is modified, compare **compiled cumulative quaternion deltas**, including multiplication order, across both sides; copying a numeric Euler offset into three files does not prove an unchanged delta.
- Even an arms-only change moves arm/hand mass. `Sway.onBind` (`:1663–1672`) rebuilds noise/schedules, resolves rig geometry, measures pendulum and stance response, then derives foot-release thresholds. `measureStanceResponse` explicitly reads whole-body mass including carried hands/feet (`:2506–2610`). Its native temporary measurements snapshot and restore the driven joints. Re-run these measurements naturally on the candidate's fresh posed figure; do not copy the accepted avatar's cached responses. Log physical changes in COM response, stance blend and foot/ground behavior. Changed responses are a consequence to assess, not automatically an instrumentation failure.

## What a full-hand-aware inward limit means here

The current vertical-humerus budget does not reserve a hand envelope. Moving the resting upper arm outward can simply enlarge its allowable inward rotation, letting anger consume the new clearance. A changed rest pose and an inward limit must therefore be evaluated together. If the authored rest hand already intersects, no nonnegative inward cap can rescue that initial pose by itself.

The proposed cap must come from geometric qualification against full relevant body and worn garment surfaces, over the complete hand/finger/forearm envelope and the intended pose histories. The retained >=.5 skin-weight triangle subset is a reproducible defect witness, not a complete envelope certificate. Region coverage, seam/boundary faces, uncertainty, positive margins and tolerances must be specified and independently checked before deriving a cap. A static side cap can only cover the qualified g050/outfit/motion domain; it is not a general collision solver.

Unlike the **attribution** B filter, a candidate implemented inside the native budget will truthfully change `appliedArmRadians` and `appliedDegrees.armSpreadLeft/Right` when clamped (`PostureLayer.js:908–947`). Gesture's shared posture yield will consequently change (`Gesture.js:794,987–996`). Preserve this normal coupling and measure it; do not keep diagnostic B's counterfactual telemetry in a proposed product correction. Existing `appliedArmRadians` reset bookkeeping remains relevant, so reset/rebind claims require real separate evidence rather than the fresh-capture guard contract.

## Comparison with composed-pose IK

`motion/IKSolver.js:309–432,570–631` already offers a numerical two-bone solve returning local post-multiply corrections without touching bones. A future arm owner could consume the composed shoulder/elbow/wrist chain after all relevant native arm/ancestor writers, then contribute shoulder/elbow corrections through the same stack. Actual declarations/order must prove it is last for those chains; reading `bone.matrixWorld` during update reads the previous commit and is unsuitable for a fast gesture.

There is an additional current API gap: `MotionStack.posePredictionSupport` rejects a query whose requesting layer declares the queried bone or an ancestor (`:731–754`, `requesting-ancestor-writer`). Thus an IK layer declaring the shoulder cannot simply use `predictWorldMatrix` to read that shoulder's prefix pose. It needs a separately verified capture-local composed-pose reader or later narrowly reviewed API change, preserving native accumulated rotation order, offsets, hierarchy and scale. Do not bypass this by temporarily writing live bones, using previous-frame matrices, or silently weakening the existing point-gaze query contract.

IK could adapt to combined posture/idle/gesture/weight-shift states beyond a static reserve. Its extra obligations are substantial: derive a whole-hand-safe target rather than only a clear wrist point; choose a stable elbow plane at bind; retain wrist/finger expressivity; handle reach/inner-radius/near-straight degeneracy and exported scale; limit correction and preserve gesture timing; and make activation/release continuous without smoothing through the forbidden surface. A late correction can also change clothing, arm silhouette and perceived intent while raw gesture telemetry still reports the uncorrected prescription. It must report both. This is a larger ownership and continuity problem than coherent authored initialization, and attribution must justify selecting it.
