# Sugata 姿 — design

**Status:** awaiting approval — research complete, all open questions resolved
**Date:** 2026-08-06

A browser-based real-time 3D avatar system that gives an AI agent a body. The agent
configures its own identity — male, female, or anywhere between — and the avatar embodies
what the agent says and how it says it, across the full range of human emotion, face and body.

## 1. What we are building, and what we are not

**Building:** an engine whose rendering and animation technique is genuinely AAA-tier, so the
quality ceiling is set by the character asset fed to it rather than by the renderer — plus an
affect and motion system that makes an AI's embodiment read as alive.

**Not building:** a replica of a specific commercial character. Stellar Blade's fidelity is
largely *art labor* — scan-derived texture stacks, artist-groomed hair, baked lighting. That
content cannot be authored from a terminal.

**Completion gate (agreed with the user):**
- **Rendering** — harsh blind critics place our renders in the same visual family as
  real-time AAA character work. Same tier, not "better than."
- **Emote** — a *decisive* win over Live2D/VTuber in blind comparison. This is winnable
  outright: Live2D is 2.5D mesh deformation with no true gaze, no real head rotation, no body
  IK, and no physics beyond hair springs.

## 2. Architectural decisions

### 2.1 WebGPU-first, WebGL2 as a degraded tier

Forced by evidence, not preference. Every AAA-defining effect — TAA with motion vectors,
SSGI, SSR, motion blur, temporal upscaling — exists **only** in three.js's TSL/WebGPU path.
`VelocityNode` is WebGPU-only, and no velocity buffer means no TAA at all. Meanwhile
`WebGPURenderer` auto-falls back to a WebGL2 backend and TSL compiles to both WGSL and GLSL,
so we author once and pay nothing for the choice.

Firefox is served the WebGL2 tier — not for availability but for performance: its WebGPU
dispatch overhead is ~1040 µs against Safari/M-series' 31.7 µs, which at ~15 passes/frame is
~15 ms of pure overhead. Disqualifying.

### 2.2 The character comes from MakeHuman/MPFB2 (CC0)

The only source satisfying all four requirements: permissive licensing, a continuous
male↔female axis, an AAA quality ceiling, and headless generation. Its gender macro is a true
continuous blend **defaulting to 0.5 — androgynous**. 14,766 all-quad faces, professional
topology.

Every alternative fails decisively: Ready Player Me shut down 2026-01-31; Character Creator 4
and Daz3D explicitly prohibit browser-delivered character generation in their EULAs; MetaHuman
cannot be redistributed openly and has no continuous gender axis; SMPL is non-commercial;
VRoid cannot reach realism because the anime proportions are in the mesh, not the shader.

**Verified, and better than expected: MPFB2 already ships canonical ARKit 52 as CC0**, in the
external `faceunits01` asset pack — plus 22 Microsoft SSML visemes and 15 Meta/OVR visemes. They
are hm08-native (max vertex index 15,119 into the 19,158-vertex base), so no retargeting is needed,
and they were authored by `met4citizen`, who also wrote the MIT three.js `TalkingHead` runtime that
already drives this exact morph namespace. **The chain is proven end-to-end, not theoretical.**

⚠️ The metadata says *"Autogenerated ARKit faceunit"*. Budget an art pass on `mouthClose`,
`mouthRollLower/Upper`, `eyeBlinkL/R` and the lid crease — exactly where automated transfer breaks.

**MPFB2 headless is a first-class supported path** (Blender ≥ 4.2, ships
`test/execute_tests_headless.bash`). The plan's highest-risk assumption is resolved.

**Licence split, resolved in our favour.** MPFB2 assets are CC0 and `LICENSE.md` §D explicitly
disclaims any claim over exported output, naming scripted export specifically. Code is GPLv3 — so
**MPFB2 is build-time only and never ships in the runtime**, which matches our offline/runtime
boundary exactly.

### 2.3 The gender axis is exactly linear — one morph pair

Measured by executing Naver's **Anny** (Apache-2.0, same hm08 topology):

```
mean |male − female| vertex displacement : 58.91 mm
max |V(0.5) − ½(V(0)+V(1))|              : 2.2e-13 mm   ← exactly linear
```

The androgynous midpoint is the exact arithmetic mean of the male and female bodies, to
floating-point precision. **So the entire identity axis ships as one morph target pair:**

```
gender_masc = V(gender=1.0) − V(gender=0.5)
gender_fem  = V(gender=0.0) − V(gender=0.5)
```

Base mesh = the androgynous midpoint. A single float reproduces every point on the axis exactly,
and composes additively with the 52 ARKit morphs. **The AI dials one number.**

This also settles the coupling question I raised in design: expressions are authored at the
androgynous midpoint, which is simultaneously the identity default *and* the position minimising
worst-case anatomical error to both extremes.

⚠️ **The skeleton caveat.** glTF cannot morph a skeleton, and head-region joints shift up to
**17.5 mm across the full sweep (≈ ±9 mm from the midpoint)** — enough to drift jaw pivot and eye
placement visibly at the extremes.

**Resolution: ship discrete baked GLBs at 3–5 gender points as the default, with the continuous
morph pair available as a live-preview mode within a clamped band.** Exact quality at the shipping
points, and a real-time dial for the AI to explore its own identity.

### 2.4 Affect is two-tier

LLM affect inference costs ~0.7 s — fine per-utterance, impossible per-frame.

- **Tier 1, reflex (< 1 ms).** Lexicon (NRC-VAD) + live prosody. Drives the face the instant
  sound starts. **Must stand alone and look right on its own, because Tier 2 is allowed to fail.**
- **Tier 2, appraisal (~0.7 s, async).** LM Studio. Returns a richer vector *blended into* the
  running state rather than snapped to, so the correction reads as settling, not popping.

This mirrors human affect — a fast automatic reaction, then a considered appraisal that colors
it. Tier 2 output is semantically validated (reject all-zero vectors, unknown emotions,
repetition collapse), not merely parsed.

### 2.5 PAD, not valence-arousal — and dominance goes to the body

Measured: sadness returns `V −0.8, A 0.3, D 0.2`; anger returns `V −0.8, A 0.7, D 0.6`. The
sharper case is anger vs. fear — same valence, same arousal, separable only by dominance.

**And dominance is not readable from a static face.** An n=109 perceptual study found pleasure
reliably identified, arousal mostly, dominance not at all: *pleasure and arousal are expressible in
the face; dominance is manifested during interaction.*

> **So the channels split: pleasure and arousal → face. Dominance → posture, gaze policy,
> interruption behaviour, gesture amplitude.** That is a structural argument for full-body rather
> than a stylistic one, and it is precisely where Live2D cannot follow.

### 2.6 Threshold and saturate, never proximity-blend

Emotions are **points** in PAD space. Activation is a radial basis function with a dead zone
(WASABI):

```
w = (1 − (d − Δ)/(Φ − Δ)) · i        d = distance to anchor, Δ = saturation, Φ = activation
```

> **Do not linearly blend all emotions weighted by proximity — that produces the average face, and
> the average face *is* the mush.** Gate so only 1–2 emotions are ever active; saturate so
> near-anchor movement doesn't flicker.

Paired with the single highest-value architectural rule: **the mouth is reserved for lipsync.**
Emotion drives brow, eye and cheek; the mouth receives only an *additive corner offset* (AU12/AU15)
on top of the viseme, never a competing absolute target. This eliminates most emotion×speech mush
for near-zero code.

### 2.7 Animate early — every timing constraint agrees

- Inline affect tags arrive ~0 ms and *ahead* of audio.
- Azure viseme blendshapes arrive batched *before* their audio chunk.
- AV-sync detectability is asymmetric: **+45 ms audio lead vs −125 ms audio lag.**
- Gesture strokes must precede the stressed syllable by 0–200 ms, so preparation starts 400–600 ms early.

Humans tolerate audio *lagging* the mouth roughly 3× more than *leading* it, because real-world
sound always arrives after sight.

> **A reactive architecture that analyses output audio is the perceptually worst case** —
> structurally one frame late and unable to anticipate. The affect signal flows forward; it is
> never recovered backward.

### 2.8 Two timescales, smoothed not switched

Never let a single utterance snap the rig. Asymmetric exponential smoothing — **fast attack
150–250 ms, slow decay 1.5–3 s** — reads as emotional inertia and buys robustness free. A much
slower mood layer sits above it (ALMA: ~10 min to change, ~20 min to return to baseline).

That two-timescale structure is what makes a character feel continuous rather than reactive.

🚩 **Open licensing item:** NRC-VAD and the Warriner norms are both **non-commercial**, which hits
the Tier-1 reflex lexicon directly. VADER (MIT) covers valence; arousal is the gap. Options:
license NRC-VAD commercially, derive arousal from acoustics alone (defensible — valence lives in
text, arousal lives in acoustics), or build a small open lexicon. **Decide before Phase 5.**

## 3. Structure

```
sugata/
├─ packages/
│  ├─ core/                    @sugata/core — the portable library
│  │  └─ src/
│  │     ├─ render/            Stage, PostChain, LightingRig, Grade
│  │     ├─ material/          SkinMaterial, EyeMaterial, HairMaterial,
│  │     │                     FabricMaterial, lut/
│  │     ├─ figure/            Figure, Identity, ExpressionBank
│  │     ├─ motion/            MotionStack, Gaze, Blink, Breath,
│  │     │                     Gesture, Posture, ik/
│  │     ├─ physics/           SpringBones, Cloth, SoftTissue
│  │     ├─ affect/            AffectState, ReflexAffect, AppraisalAffect,
│  │     │                     ExpressionMap
│  │     ├─ voice/             Speech, Visemes, Prosody
│  │     ├─ ear/               Mic, Backchannel
│  │     └─ Avatar.js          the public API
│  └─ testbed/                 demo app, LM Studio chat, critic harness
├─ tools/
│  ├─ figure-pipeline/         Blender: MPFB2 → ARKit 52 → bake → glTF/KTX2
│  ├─ lut-bake/                SSS and eye-caustic LUT bakers
│  └─ critic/                  headless capture + blind A/B harness
└─ docs/
```

Application-layer directories at the top so the architecture is implied by the tree;
domain-named files inside them. Three layers where there is state: the runtime API is decoupled
from motion and affect, which are decoupled from rendering.

**Offline vs. runtime is a hard boundary.** `tools/figure-pipeline` runs in Blender and emits
glTF + KTX2. The runtime never sees Blender, MakeHuman, or a `.mhm` file. That keeps the
shipped library small and lets the asset pipeline evolve independently.

## 4. The public API

The whole point of the library. An agent should need one call.

```js
const avatar = await Avatar.create({
  canvas,
  identity: { gender: 0.5, age: 0.35, build: 0.4, height: 0.5 },
  quality:  'auto',            // auto | cinematic | balanced | efficient
});

// The agent speaks. Affect inference, prosody, visemes, gesture,
// posture and gaze all follow from this one call.
await avatar.say("Oh — I actually didn't expect that to work.");

// Or drive affect directly, when the agent knows its own state.
avatar.feel({ valence: 0.8, arousal: 0.7, dominance: 0.6 });

// Listening: gaze, backchannel nods, listening posture.
avatar.listen(micStream);
```

`say()` is the entire required surface. An agent never has to know what a blendshape is.
`feel()` exists because an agent with its own affect model — Ember, for instance — should be
able to drive the body directly rather than having its state re-inferred from its own words.

`identity` is a continuous vector, not a preset enum. `gender: 0.5` is a real, renderable
body, not a fallback.

## 5. Rendering approach

**Pipeline** (one geometry draw, no prepass):

```
pass(scene, camera) with MRT { output, diffuseColor, normal, velocity, sssMask }
  → GTAO  → bent normal + specular occlusion  (hand-rolled; three.js has neither)
  → SSGI  (SSILVB)
  → SSR
  → TAAU  (temporal AA + upscale in one pass, resolutionScale ≈ 0.66)
  → bloom → DOF → grade (AgX) → film grain
```

`sssMask` rides as one extra named MRT channel written **only** by the skin material, via
`material.mrtNode`. Other materials write nothing.

**TAAU is the key lever.** Temporal upscaling from ~0.66 resolution is what buys us the budget
for an expensive skin shader at 60 fps. No ML upscaler exists in-browser; TAAU is the ceiling.

**Materials.** Skin: pre-integrated (Penner) SSS with a *baked* curvature map — runtime
curvature from screen-space derivatives is too noisy on a skinned, morphing face — plus
dual-lobe specular and a tiled micro-normal. Separable SSSS is a later quality tier, deferred
until the MRT G-buffer is settled. Eyes: cornea refraction into a flat iris plane, shader-side
pupil dilation (an arousal readout, free), view-dependent limbal ring, baked caustic LUT, and
the dual-normal sclera/iris trick where specular snaps flat inside the iris. Hair: Karis's
closed-form BSDF, cards as the shipping default with strands as a WebGPU-only LOD0.

**Lighting.** 3–4 RectAreaLights (key/fill/rim/kicker — a classic portrait rig) for the soft
wrapped specular that is the single biggest AAA tell. They cast no shadows, so each pairs with
a co-located shadow-casting directional. Screen-space contact shadows on top for eyelid crease,
nostril and lip corner. Studio HDRI via PMREM at low intensity, rotatable independently of the
backdrop. A hand-placed per-eye catchlight, non-negotiable for portrait framing.

**Two known gaps we must hand-roll:** three.js has no bent normals and no specular occlusion
(un-occluded ambient specular is the commonest reason WebGL characters look like plastic), and
its specular anti-aliasing is geometric only — normal-map detail contributes nothing to
roughness, so micro-detail and hair will shimmer violently without a normal-variance term.

## 6. Phases

**Order revised after research.** The highest perceptual return per unit of effort is the ocular
and idle layer, and it needs neither the affect pipeline nor the good shaders — only a face to sit
on. Building it early gets something demonstrably *alive* on the plain base mesh, and lets the
emote-vs-Live2D critic loop run **in parallel with** renderer work rather than after it.

| # | Phase | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Foundation — scaffold, asset pipeline, critic harness, **spikes** | Pipeline emits a GLB with 52 named ARKit morphs |
| 1 | Body and identity — gender morph pair, ARKit bank, rig | Gender axis renders correctly end to end |
| 2 | **Ocular + idle** — blink, saccade, VOR, breath, sway | **Reads as alive when silent and unshaded** |
| 3 | Rendering — skin, eyes, hair, cloth, lighting, post | Blind critic: same tier; 6 measured gates pass |
| 4 | Speech — viseme timeline, TTS, coarticulation | Lipsync convincing, scheduled early |
| 5 | Affect — PAD state, WASABI activation, AU mapping, mic-in | Full emotional range legible |
| 6 | Body motion — gesture, posture, IK, physics | Dominance readable from the body |
| 7 | Runtime API and testbed | An agent embeds it in one call |
| 8 | Blind critic loops | Both gates met |

### Phase 0 spikes — run before any architecture depends on them

1. ✅ **MPFB2 headless — resolved by verification.** First-class supported path, ships
   `test/execute_tests_headless.bash`. Still prove our *specific* chain end-to-end:
   set gender → load ARKit faceunits → bake → export GLB → load in three.js with morphs named.
2. **Morph-target cost.** three.js iterates a `DataArrayTexture` layer per target — cost is
   O(vertices × targets) per frame. Benchmark 52 + visemes + gender pair on the 13.7k-quad base.
   Directly sets the blendshape budget.
3. **Hair performance.** Open the frostbitten-hair WebGPU demo on this Mac, use its built-in
   profiler, sweep the strand slider. Twenty minutes, and it replaces every extrapolated
   estimate with a measurement.
4. **RectAreaLight cost.** Where does the portrait rig actually start costing us?
5. **Visual check on the autogenerated faceunits** at gender and age extremes — the one quality
   unknown in the asset chain.
6. **Diff Anny vs MPFB2 vertex ordering** before assuming faceunits transfer without remapping.

### The six objective critic gates

From measurement of official reference stills — these run in `tools/critic` **before** any
subjective judgment:

1. Face key:shadow luma ratio **< 2:1** (the reference is flat-lit at 1.25:1)
2. Sclera renders at **~0.98× cheek luma**, not white
3. Skin shadow terminator gets **more saturated and redder**, not bluer (an SSS correctness test)
4. Flat-skin high-pass σ ≈ **1.5–2.1 / 255** at 4K
5. **< 0.5%** of pixels clipped above 0.99 luma
6. Black point at **p0.1 luma ≈ 0.004–0.016** — no lift

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| ~~MPFB2 cannot run headless~~ | ✅ **Resolved** — first-class supported path, verified. |
| Autogenerated faceunits are too coarse for close-up | Budget an art pass on lip seal and lid crease. Cross-check against the in-repo CC0 FACS units. Visual test is Phase 0 spike #5. |
| hm08 is a 2010s mesh; quality ceiling | Accepted. **Escape hatch: Google's GNM Head** (Apache-2.0, quads + UVs, 383-dim regional expression basis, modelled eyes/teeth/gums/tongue) if photoreal close-up becomes the binding constraint. |
| Texture quality is the real ceiling | Accepted and stated up front. Lee Perry-Smith CC BY maps for detail; budget one-time artist effort. |
| Skeleton can't morph with gender (±9 mm head-joint drift) | Ship discrete baked GLBs; continuous dial clamped to a narrow band for live preview. |
| Specular shimmer on micro-detail | Hand-rolled normal-variance roughness + TRAA. Verify early with a **moving camera**, not a still. |
| Hair is the largest single block | Cards first, strands as LOD0. Karis BSDF shared between both paths. |
| Asset budget (~90 MB VRAM per 4K map) | KTX2, UASTC for data maps, ETC1S for albedo. First hard wall on mobile. |
| Affect lexicon is non-commercial | Decide before Phase 5 — commercial licence, acoustics-only arousal, or build an open lexicon. |
| Critic loop never terminates | Gate is "same tier," not "better," **plus six objectively measured properties** that must pass before subjective judgment is even solicited. |
| The niche is contested | Warudo embeds a browser with a JS bridge; 3tene ships an MCP server. **Our differentiator is not rendering — it's that we have the intent/affect stream natively.** AlterEcho (N=315) showed procedural semantic animation beats plain mocap; every incumbent is a *tracker*. |

## 8. Open items

All research is complete. Remaining decisions, each deferred to the phase that needs it:

- **Affect lexicon licensing** (before Phase 5) — the one item with a commercial consequence.
- **TTS engine.** Needs viseme or phoneme timing. Leading candidate: **HeadTTS** (MIT) runs
  Kokoro-82M ONNX in-browser and returns words, times, visemes *and* phonemes — client-side, no
  eSpeak, no GPL. Alternatives with phoneme timestamps: Cartesia, Inworld. Azure ships 60 fps
  ARKit-ordered blendshapes directly.
- **Whether corrective shapes at the gender extremes are needed** — measure first.
- **Whether to ship separable SSSS** as a quality tier above pre-integrated.
- **Whether to adopt the UPF-GTI web-native BML stack** (`eBMLController`, `IK-threejs`,
  `retargeting-threejs` — Apache-2.0/MIT, three.js-native, and `faceEmotion` already accepts a
  `valaro: [valence, arousal]` input) or build the motion layer directly.

**Explicitly out of scope for v1**, with reasoning: ML co-speech gesture generation (nobody has run
one in a browser; do retrieval over an offline-generated motion library instead — that's sub-100 ms
today with zero ONNX work), cloth simulation beyond spring bones, and webcam/mocap tracking.
