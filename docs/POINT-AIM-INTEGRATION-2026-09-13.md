# Eye focus integrated into the live attention action

Portrait’s experimental **Look toward me** action now aims the two eyes at the sampled camera point through the native gaze controller. It retains the existing head trajectory, natural small eye movements, and eyelid/brow following. Release eases the correction away; a newer gaze or speech command takes ownership immediately. This is a measured improvement to directed attention, not completed humanlike expression or AAA visual quality.

[Try the long-bob portrait](http://127.0.0.1:5197/src/portrait.html?hair=bob01) or [compare twelve recorded moments](http://127.0.0.1:5197/@fs/Users/robault/GitHub/sugata-avatar/captures/point-aim-integration-2026-09-13/gallery/review.html). ## Runtime behavior

`Avatar` builds an eye profile for each loaded figure from its neutral geometry and four measured unit morph responses per eye. The supported geometry has relative, independent eyeLook morphs and rigid head skinning. All five shipped figure calibrations pass CPU checks. The broad physical approximation and solver qualification remains the g050 study; the additional figures have calibration validity checks, not equivalent visual/range qualification.

`Gaze.holdTarget(direction, { pointWorld, pointWeight })` copies an optional finite world point. Existing calls without a point retain their prior behavior. Invalid point requests are validated before superseding a valid hold. Gaze obtains a read-only prediction of the current frame’s head pose from MotionStack and writes its own declared eye channels before the single normal commit. The layer must be active at full weight, own its eye channels exclusively, and have no requesting/later ancestor bone writer. Unsupported geometry, ownership or transforms retain directional behavior with an explicit fallback status. An action started while support is unavailable uses the directional path.

A point hold exposes `active`, `hasPointTarget`, `pointFading`, `setPointWeight(0..1)` and `release({pointFadeSeconds:0..0.5})`. Immediate release is the default. Soft release restores policy immediately and leaves a token-bound eye fade; an old handle can stop only its own residual fade. It cannot restart it, clear a newer hold, or change a newer policy. Attention uses a 0.3-second soft release. Shared eye yaw/pitch represent the mean final authored per-eye angles before FacialIdle evaluates lid and brow following. `modelResidualDegrees` describes the unblended solver fit, not the actual blended image’s camera error.

The bounded solver preserves the previous native horizontal policy: In at most 0.821225457, Out/Up/Down at most 0.85, corresponding to 12.1295 degrees of nominal horizontal reach. A constrained miss reports `limited`. This is software movement policy, not a physiological comfort claim. Affine transforms must be positive uniform similarities; arbitrary altered rigs are not supported.

## Two defects proved and repaired

The first candidate inherited a generic removal bug: MotionStack dropped the last owner’s channels before committing rest. Actual g050 eye morphs remained exactly frozen after Gaze removal despite an inactive handle. Channels now survive through one zero/rest commit, then are relinquished. Re-adding a writer before that commit retains the original bone rest; binding a different target drops the old target’s pending retirement instead of writing unowned channels on the replacement. Gaze publishes zero follower angles on removal. The critic’s alternative was a required disable/update/remove protocol; the implemented retirement keeps the existing direct-removal contract.

The first browser run then rejected unchanged calibration after GPU upload. Installed Three.js widens the original unnormalized Uint8/Uint16 skin-index array to Uint32, preserving every value, attribute identity, version and layout. Both the real browser and the critic’s execution of the installed upload code prove identical recalibrated profiles. The guard now accepts only that exact lossless conversion, once. Changed indices, versions, layouts and other replaced arrays still reject. The critic’s alternative was eagerly normalizing the figure’s indices before calibration; the selected guard avoids that extra figure mutation. Normal Three version invalidation remains required for in-place array edits.

## Qualification and preserved failures

The paired browser action completes 240 steps over four seconds, with twelve PNG/state samples per arm and 189 corrected trace frames. Recorded head, native gaze, hair, camera and clock controls match exactly. Action reports match except the new eye-focus status. Every corrected predicted head/rig matrix equals its later committed matrix; final reported eye weights equal the exclusive Gaze commit. Initial and returned samples at steps 205 and 240 restore the recorded eye/body-eye morphs and posed eye clouds exactly.

At 1.5 seconds, fitted corneal-axis camera error changes from 6.311/6.209 degrees to 0.4824/0.4829 degrees. This retains the roughly half-degree natural eye offset. The primary reviewer inspected original/integrated hold and integrated onset/release images: a modestly more direct gaze, without an obvious new ocular failure in those stills. Corneal-axis error is a geometry proxy, not a calibrated perceptual eye-contact score. Hair cards and overall facial finish remain open.

The paired run’s overall report remains `completed:false`: a subsequent lifecycle assertion incorrectly expected immediate support after the first reset frame. The diagnostic proves calibration stays current while the existing IdleMotion arm declaration is pending. A separate lifecycle-only run explicitly checks that safe first-frame deferral, then support on the next frame, and completes soft cancel, speech supersession, repeat/stale handles, immediate cleanup, reset, g075 identity replacement and fresh aim, disable-to-rest, and removal-to-rest. The lifecycle driver inherits paired-harness introductory prose; its actual scope is its nine recorded checks over approximately 6.08 seconds, plus one initial PNG/state sample. It is not another four-second paired comparison. Three successful action/lifecycle Avatar instances retire with every native memory counter zero and no leaked handles.

The initial upload rejection, reset-expectation failure, original physical-removal red control, and corrected probe mistakes are retained.
